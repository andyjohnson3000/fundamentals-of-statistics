# Fundamentals of Statistics

A textbook on the mathematics of statistical inference, by A. Reeves Johnson, for readers who have taken a solid undergraduate statistics course and a semester of calculus.

**Read the current edition:** [main.pdf](main.pdf). [EDITIONS.md](EDITIONS.md) lists every edition with a link to its PDF; `v0.11` means Chapters 1 to 11 are written.

## What the book covers

Statistical models; the Law of Large Numbers, the Central Limit Theorem and the tools for working with limits; the Gaussian distribution and quantiles; estimators, confidence intervals and hypothesis tests; distances between distributions; maximum likelihood and its properties; the method of moments and M-estimation; parametric tests (Student's t, Wald, likelihood ratio); goodness of fit and Kolmogorov-Smirnov tests; Bayesian statistics; linear regression; generalized linear models; and principal component analysis. All the limit mathematics is present and proved, with intuition, figures, worked examples and connections to introductory statistics throughout.

The material follows the content of MIT's 18.6501x, Fundamentals of Statistics. The text, figures and examples are the author's own; no course materials are included in this repository.

## Building the PDF

With a TeX distribution installed (MacTeX on a Mac):

```bash
latexmk -pdf main.tex
```

To regenerate the figures from their scripts (Python 3 with matplotlib and numpy):

```bash
python3 scripts/make_figures.py
```

`make` does both; `make clean` removes the LaTeX build files.

## What is in this repository

| Path | What it is |
|---|---|
| `main.tex`, `preamble.tex`, `notes_macros.tex` | The book's top file, its layout and boxes, and its notation macros |
| `chapters/` | One file per chapter and appendix |
| `figures/` | Every figure, as PDF, generated by the scripts |
| `scripts/` | One script per figure, plus the shared style (`scripts/_style.py`) |
| `data/` | Real datasets the figures use |
| `texmf/` | Two small font packages and the `answers` package, for TeX installations that lack them |
| `STYLE.md` | How the book is written: the reader, the devices every chapter uses, and the conventions |
